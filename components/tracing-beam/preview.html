The top

Every long page makes the same promise at the top: this will be worth it. The beam starts empty because the reader has not yet decided.

The middle

Halfway down, most readers are skimming for the part that matters to them. A filled rail says the end is a known distance away, which is often enough to keep them reading carefully instead.

The turn

Somewhere in the third quarter the page has to pay off its opening promise. Structure helps: short sections, honest headings, and nothing that exists only to make the page longer.

The end

When the dot reaches the bottom, the reader has the whole argument. What they do next — copy a part, open the registry, close the tab — was decided by the words, but the beam walked them there.
